Autonomous Lundy passage — project plan

A 1 m RC sailing hull retrofitted with the autopilot we just ported to Rust, building toward an unattended crossing from Ilfracombe to Lundy Island. Last updated .

What we have

  • 6-DOF Rust simulator, ported from Simon Kohaut's Python. Force model, ODE integration (Dopri5), heading controller (PID/LQR), sail-trim optimiser, route follower.
  • Cached coastline chart for Lundy + the North Devon coast (Saunton → Heddon's Mouth), fetched from OpenStreetMap and projected to a tangent-plane local metre frame centred on the island.
  • Two reference routes: a Lundy circumnavigation and the full Ilfracombe round-trip shown below.
  • Autopilot trait separating the control stack from the sim — the same RouteAutopilot code will run unchanged on hardware once a sensor/servo runner is written.

The seam between control and world

Every outer control tick the runner builds an Observation and hands it to autopilot.step(obs), which returns a Command. Neither side knows whether the other is sim or hardware:

pub trait Autopilot {
    fn step(&mut self, obs: &Observation) -> Command;
    fn reset(&mut self) {}
}

Hull: second-hand 1 m IOM

The International One Metre class is the most popular RC sailing class in the UK and is well-suited to retrofit:

  • 1000 mm LOA, ~4 kg displacement, deep bulb keel → self-rights from a knockdown.
  • Three standard rigs (A, B, C) for different wind ranges; you swap rigs, not boats.
  • Already designed to carry rudder servo + sail winch + receiver, so hardpoints exist.
  • Active second-hand market via UK MYA clubs, eBay, Facebook Marketplace — older hulls (Italiko, Triple Crown, TS2, Goth) routinely change hands for £50–£200.

What to check on a used hull

  • Hull integrity: no cracks at the keel box, no soft spots, deck patch repairs OK if dry.
  • Rig included: at least one set of A rig (mast, boom, jib boom, sails). Replacements are pricey.
  • Servo bay watertight: gasketed hatch is essential for coastal trials.
  • Sail winch: a drum-style winch (RMG, Hitec HS-785HB) is much better than an arm servo for autonomy — smoother and full range.
  • Keel bulb intact: this is the self-righting moment; chipped/cracked bulbs leak lead.
Honest scoping. An IOM is a pond/lake racer. With good weather windows and a chase kayak it can do sheltered coastal trials (Ilfracombe inner harbour, Combe Martin bay, the inside of Hartland Point), but the full ~30 km open-water passage to Lundy is a stretch goal that may want a bigger hull in the end. The sim work, the autopilot, the electronics — all of it carries straight across to a larger boat later. Treat the IOM as the platform that gets the control stack debugged.

On the speed calibration. The simulator is a faithful port of Kohaut et al. (2018), whose stated scope is dynamic behaviour, not absolute speed — the paper says “the precise prediction of the actual reachable velocities are of minor interest” and that c_wr and the damping coefficients “still need” identification from recorded data. The current IOM speeds (~1.1 m/s upwind, ~1.2 m/s reaching at 4 m/s wind) come from curve-fitting c_wr to published typical IOM figures, so the polar shape and magnitudes are plausible but not validated. One afternoon logging GPS speed-vs-wind-angle on the real boat (phase 6) turns this into a proper fit. Above ~5 m/s the model also goes unstable (overpowered / heel-control), so treat it as calibrated for moderate conditions only.

Where it could fail

  • Sim-vs-real boat dynamics mismatch. The current sim is tuned for a 4 m hull. The IOM is much lighter and faster-responding. Expect to retune the PID gains and possibly the sail-trim stall angle.
  • GPS jitter under sail. A 2 Hz consumer GPS gives positions accurate to ~2 m, fine on the Bristol Channel scale but coarse for harbour manoeuvring. The XTE lookahead in the route follower will need re-tuning.
  • Wind sensing. True-wind solving from apparent + GPS course is noisy at low boat speed. Plan a fallback that uses a slowly-filtered apparent-wind reading directly.
  • Knockdown recovery. The IOM self-rights, but the sail winch needs a defined neutral position so the boat doesn't capsize again under load. Add a "knockdown detected → release sheets" rule before any unsupervised run.
  • Range & retrieval. Even 1 km offshore in a IOM is a long way to swim after if the radio link drops. Failsafe = drop sails + GPS-log SOS, but realistically you want a chase boat within sight.
